Post Generator

How to turn what food-industry organisations publish on LinkedIn into an editorial post and a carousel you have read, edited and approved yourself.

What it makes

Two things, from the same material:

  • a post — the LinkedIn text: a headline, the body, a closing line and hashtags;
  • a carousel — a set of square 1080×1080 images you swipe through, plus the words that accompany them.

They come from the same evidence: recent posts by the organisations you follow, scored for relevance, grouped into topics, and written up.

The tool never publishes anything. It produces files and text; you post them yourself. Nothing leaves the tool without a person pressing Approve.

The words on the images are yours, letter for letter. The text on every slide is drawn by the tool from the text you approved. No model rewrites it into the picture. When the AI option is used, the model produces only the background behind the words.

Signing in

Open the address you were given and enter your email and password. There is no self-registration: an account is created for you.

If you forget your password there is no self-service reset — ask the person who gave you the account.

The six screens

The navigation bar reads left to right, and that order is the process. Each screen feeds the next, so skipping one leaves the next screen empty.

Sources→Settings→Rating→ Topics→Review & Approve→Export
ScreenWhat you do thereWhat comes out
SourcesChoose which LinkedIn pages are watched, and collect their recent postsCollected posts
SettingsSet the editorial direction: themes, the relevance bar, the brief, tone and audienceThe rules everything else follows
RatingScore the collected posts for relevance and see why each got its scoreScored posts
TopicsHide company names, group similar posts into topics, then create the post and carouselA draft

ExportDownload the final text as Word, Markdown or JSONFiles

Two words that sound alike and are not. Themes are the five or six subjects you score against, set in Settings — food safety, supply chain, and so on. Topics are groups of similar posts, worked out automatically, and each one becomes a section of the publication and a slide in the carousel. Themes are what you are looking for; topics are what was found.

Step one

Sources

The list of LinkedIn pages the tool reads, and where new material comes in.

The columns

  • Lookback — how many days back this page is read when you collect. Anyone can change it.
  • Last fetched — when this page was last read successfully.
  • Enabled — the switch. A page that is off is skipped by Collect all enabled. Turning it off is reversible and is the polite way to pause a source.

Collecting

Press Collect on one row to read that page, or Collect all enabled at the top right for every active page. It takes a few seconds; the button says so while it runs. You then get a short result, for example “4 new, 46 outside your lookback window” — the second number is the one to read if nothing new arrived: the posts existed, they were simply older than your lookback.

Find names

Find names asks the model to read this page's recent posts and suggest product or subsidiary names worth hiding — brands the anonymiser would not work out from the page name alone. Everything it returns is a proposal: nothing is hidden until you accept it.

Collect and Find names both cost money. Collect sends a real request to the LinkedIn data provider; Find names asks a language model. Neither is free, so collect when you mean to, not to see what happens.

Step two

Settings

The editorial direction. Nothing here does anything on its own — it changes what the other screens do the next time you run them. The screen is grouped by which screen each setting actually reaches, and each group says so.

What matters most

  • Themes — the subjects every post is scored against. Change these and you change what the tool considers relevant at all.
  • Relevance threshold — the bar a post must clear. It reaches two screens: a post below it is excluded from the topic grouping and from the final text.
  • Editorial brief — the instructions to the model about what this publication is and what it should say. This is the single most important field on the screen. Write it as you would brief a colleague: for example, “emphasise the factors that shape how consumers choose healthy and sustainable food”.
  • Tone and Audience — two closed lists that shift the register of the writing. They are lists rather than free text so that a typo cannot quietly change the result.
  • Company and brand names — the names replaced wherever they appear. This is where accepted Find names suggestions end up.

Press Save at the top right. An unsaved changes marker appears beside it while anything is unsaved.

Step three

Rating

Everything collected, scored for how well it fits your themes, with the reasoning shown.

Reading a row

  • Overall — one number out of 100. It is the highest single theme score, not an average: a post that is excellent on one theme and irrelevant to the rest still scores high, which is deliberate.
  • Per-theme scores — the same post measured against each theme separately.
  • The paragraph under the title is the model's own explanation of the score. Read it when a number surprises you.
  • 1 of 6 themes, in orange — how many of your themes this post cleared the relevance threshold on. A post showing a low count is narrow, not necessarily weak.
  • in generation, in green — this post is eligible to be used in the final text.

Scoring

Press Score now. One press scores up to ten posts. If more are waiting, press it again — the orange badge tells you how many are left. The list refreshes itself when a batch finishes.

Re-score all scores everything again from scratch. Use it after you change the themes or the brief, not routinely.

If the screen looks empty, do not assume something broke. Either nothing was collected in the period you are looking at, or it was collected but not yet scored. The screen tells you which, and gives you the button that fixes it.

Step four

Topics

Three things happen here, in order, and the screen is laid out in that order.

First — hide the company names

Press Anonymise now. This rewrites the collected posts so that company and brand names are replaced with neutral descriptions, using the list in Settings. The publication is written from these rewritten posts, never from the originals.

Redo all repeats it for everything, including posts already done. Use it only after changing the name list in Settings.

Second — group them into topics

Choose a period start and period end and press Run clustering. Posts that say similar things are grouped together; each group is a topic.

Why group at all? Because a carousel is a sequence of sections, not a list of links. Grouping is what turns scattered posts into an argument with parts. Each topic becomes one section of the post and one slide of the carousel.

The Viewing run dropdown lets you look back at an earlier grouping. Posts marked unclustered did not resemble anything else closely enough to join a group; they are simply not used this time.

Third — create it

The blue box tells you exactly what you are about to make: the period it covers, how many themes became sections, and how many slides that is. Press Create the carousel.

You get one post and one carousel covering that period. The slide count is the number of topics plus an opening and a closing slide.

Anonymising, grouping and creating all use a language model and all cost money. They are cheap individually. Redo all is the expensive one, because it repeats the work for every post.

When it finishes, the draft is waiting in Review & Approve.

Step five

Review & Approve

The screen where the work actually gets judged. It opens with the finished thing, not with a form.

The list on the left holds every draft. Each entry says whether it is a post or a carousel, its status, and whether it has been edited.

Looking through the carousel

Use ‹ Previous and Next ›, or click any thumbnail in the strip, to move between slides. Click a slide to enlarge it.

Editing

Under the preview you edit the slide currently on screen — its heading and its body. Change a word and the slide above redraws a moment later, so you see the result rather than imagining it. While it is redrawing, the download buttons are disabled: you can never save a file that does not match the words in front of you.

Post text — the three fields underneath

FieldDrawn on the images?Where it appears
TitleYesIn the footer of every slide except the first, and as the first heading in the exported file
CaptionNoThe words posted alongside the images, and in the export
CTANoThe closing line under the caption, and in the export

Press Save edits. It turns dark with white text, and an unsaved edits marker appears, as soon as you change anything. Nothing you type is kept until you press it — and the Export screen shows the last saved version.

Approving or rejecting

Optionally write a line in “Why you approved or rejected this”. It is exactly what it says: a note to yourself, stored with your decision and visible only when you reopen this item. Nothing else reads it and it does not influence future drafts.

Then press Approve or Reject. Approving is what makes a draft available on the Export screen.

Asking for a different draft

Ask for a new draft writes a fresh version with your note and the current draft in front of the model — for example “too corporate, lead with the enforcement angle”. Leave the box empty for a different take on the same evidence. The existing draft is kept: nothing is overwritten, you get a second one alongside it.

Downloading the images

This is the step that produces what you actually upload to LinkedIn. You choose the background:

OptionWhat it isCost
Designed templateA designed background drawn by the tool itself. Immediate, no AI involved.Free
AI background imageOne generated picture per slide, based on that slide's text, with your words drawn on top.Billed per image

Nothing is generated until you press the button, and the button tells you how many images it will actually buy. Once a picture exists, changing the wording redraws your text on the same picture at no extra cost — only Redo on a single slide buys a new one. Quality is offered as fastest, medium and slowest; higher quality takes longer and costs more.

Generating takes a little while and the screen says so throughout — Drawing…, Generating…, Redrawing for your edits…. Then Download saves one PNG per slide, 1080×1080, named in reading order.

Step six

Export

The final text, as files.

  • Status filters what you see. It starts on approved, because that is normally what you want to send out.
  • MD / JSON / DOCX choose the format — Markdown, raw data, or a Word document.
  • Select an entry to preview it, or press Download all.

Export shows your edits; it is the saved version that counts. If you edited a draft in Review and pressed Save, the export contains the edited text, not the model's original. If you edited and did not press Save, it contains the previous version.

The images are not here. They are downloaded on the Review & Approve screen, because they are made from the text you are looking at there. Export is for the words.

The four status words

StatusMeaning
draftWritten by the tool, not yet judged by a person. The normal starting state.
approvedA person read it and accepted it. This is what Export shows by default.
rejectedA person read it and decided it should not go out. It is kept, not deleted.
supersededA newer draft answered this one — you asked for a new version. The older one is kept so the history stays readable, and is hidden by default.

Nothing is ever destroyed by these decisions. A rejected draft and the model's original wording both remain, which is what makes it possible to see later what was written, what was changed, and by whom.

What you can change

There are two levels of access. Both see everything and both can run the whole process end to end. The difference is a short list of actions that add, rename or permanently delete.

ActionOrdinary user

Change any setting on the Settings screenYes
Change a source's lookback, or switch it on and offYes
Add a new sourceNo — ask the team
Rename a source or change its addressNo

During the trial, sources are added for you. If you want a page watched, ask — it takes a minute.

If something looks wrong

A screen is empty

Almost always the previous step has not been run, or the period you are looking at has nothing in it. Each screen says which of the two it is. Widen the period, or go back one screen.

A red message appears

Read it — it says what happened. If it mentions permissions, it is one of the admin-only actions in the table above. If it mentions the provider, the request did not get through and trying again later is reasonable. Messages disappear after a few seconds; if you missed one, repeat the action and read it this time.

Nothing new was collected

Look at the second half of the result message. “46 outside your lookback window” means posts were found but were older than the lookback set for that source. Raise the lookback and collect again.

Find names returned nothing

That is a normal answer, not a fault. It means nothing new was proposed beyond what is already in the list — or there were no collected posts in that source's window to read.

The images do not match what I just typed

They will, a moment after you stop typing. While a redraw is pending the screen says Redrawing for your edits… and the download buttons are disabled on purpose. Wait for the count to read ready.
